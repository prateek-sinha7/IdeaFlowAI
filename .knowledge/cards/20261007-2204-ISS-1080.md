## Condition

Anyone reading the "Why this shape" section of `.knowledge/architecture/DOMAIN-durable-state-resume.md` to learn why the engine's event
sink keeps its own in-memory seq counter instead of allocating through `ScopedStore.append_event_next_seq`.

## The text (CONFIRMED, read)

`.knowledge/architecture/DOMAIN-durable-state-resume.md:395-399`:

> **Two constraints instead of a lock, because there is genuinely a second writer.** The engine's sink keeps a fast in-memory seq counter
> and cannot afford to materialize the whole log per event (`_max_event_seq` exists precisely because `read_events` pulls every row —
> the local corpus has a 36k-row run). Serializing all writers through one allocator was the obvious alternative and was rejected on that
> cost.

"That cost" is a whole-log read per event. `append_event_next_seq` stopped paying it in FIX-688: each attempt is one keyed `event_id`
lookup plus one `_max_event_seq` probe, and `read_events` is not called (`authz.py:556-575`; probe
`evidence/qa-kb-074/02-probe-cost-of-append_event_next_seq.txt`). The method's own docstring states the live reason the sink keeps its
counter, `authz.py:541-543`: "(that would add two index probes per event)".

## Why the root cause implies it

It is the claim in `ScopedStore._max_event_seq`'s docstring (`authz.py:595-598`, "and is why the engine sink cannot allocate through
`append_event_next_seq`"), one tier up. The figure "36k-row run" appears in only that docstring, in the `_stamp_resume_marker` docstring
(`engine.py:13085-13088`) and in this paragraph (`evidence/qa-kb-074/03-probe-stale-prose-sites.txt`), which suggests the paragraph was
written from the docstring (INFERRED: no log records it). If so, fixing the docstrings fixes the source of the sentence but not the
sentence.

## What stays true in the paragraph

"The engine's sink keeps a fast in-memory seq counter", "`_max_event_seq` exists precisely because `read_events` pulls every row", and
the two-constraints design (migration 0024, both writers retry past a collision) are all still correct. Only "cannot afford to
materialize the whole log per event" and "rejected on that cost" describe a cost that is gone.
