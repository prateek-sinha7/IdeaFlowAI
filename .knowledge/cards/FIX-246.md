## Description

The historical event loss was recorded as an unmeasured range with a half-wrong justification (ISS-123); it is now a read-only, re-runnable audit. Shipped backend/scripts/audit_lost_run_events.py — mode=ro, zero INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE/ALTER. It simulates the engine seq allocator rather than looking for holes, because a gap proves nothing: the stealing app writer OCCUPIES the seq, so a steal destroys an engine event only when the engine's counter actually WALKS onto it. Two loss signatures: WALKED-ONTO, and ORPHAN-CARD — a narrator chat_reply:{uuid} card whose source uuid has no row, written by the always-lands allocator so it outlives its own source and acts as a tombstone that NAMES the destroyed event. Corrects the register: 15 destroyed events across 9 runs, not ~29 across 10 (agent_chunk 11, questionnaire_complete 2, review_gate_approved 1, pipeline_cancelled 1) — the 29 counted seq-stealing app ROWS, an upper bound, not the loss. Also corrects the reason for refusing back-fill. "A dropped row has no provenance to reconstruct from" is FALSE for 11 of the 15: they are agent_chunk fragments and artifact_refs.content is an independent authoritative record of the same text — the audit sizes each hole against it and reproduces the lost strings exactly by length (1 = the document's leading #, 4, 14, 26, 27). The correct reason is narrower and stronger: the CONTENT is recoverable, the IDENTITY is not. event_id is the key the whole replay contract rests on (replayed_event_ids, append_event_next_seq idempotency, the FE seenRef), so a back-fill must mint an event_id that never existed — making the log assert the engine emitted a frame it did not, with a de-dup key no live frame can match. The four structural losses are unrecoverable outright: workflow_clarifications is EMPTY corpus-wide and gate_events records NO approve outcome for any run. Three self-corrections are documented IN the script because each would silently inflate or misattribute the damage: post-FIX-240 runs show the identical walked-onto shape but lost nothing (the row was re-appended at max+1 — only creation time discriminates, hence --fix240-utc, and counting them gave 16/10); a destroyed review_gate_approved reads as "unknown" unless the scan looks back PAST the narrator card that always follows a review_gate_ready; and sizing a hole against the SUM of artifact_refs rows double-counts a re-run agent's versions, reporting a 1-character hole as 18,718.

## Root cause

ISS-123 was filed from a steal count rather than a loss simulation, and its recoverability claim was asserted rather than tested — so the damage was overstated by ~2x and the strongest argument against repair was never stated.
