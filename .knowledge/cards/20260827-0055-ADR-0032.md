## Decision

There are three points where a run stops and waits on a person: a review gate, a
routed choice gate, and a clarification round. All three now open with the
question.

## Why ask-first, and not read-first

The card renders BETWEEN agent rows in the Steps spine — it never owns the
viewport. Putting the decision at the bottom buries it mid-list with more rows
below; anchoring it as a fixed footer needs viewport ownership it does not have.
Ask-first puts the decision where the eye lands as the card scrolls into view,
and it is the only order that keeps the full question text: the alternatives that
share a row with the actions force the ask to be shortened to fit, and the ask
comes off the wire.

## The cost, and what pays it

Ask-first invites approving without scrolling the evidence. Two borrowings
mitigate it: the ask sits in its own brand-filled block with a rule (so the
decision is a place, not a heading), and the decision block casts a shadow down
onto a well whose top edge is shaded (so the well reads as passing underneath,
with more to it). The well being a capped preview rather than the reading surface
is what makes that acceptable — a 12k-character spec was never going to be read
in a 220px window, and the whole artifact is one tab away.

## Consequences

- The Steps spine no longer wraps these cards in its own bordered card with a
  header row; two frames and two headers pushed the question below the fold.
- `short_text` and `hybrid` clarify questions gained the free-text input the
  backend has always emitted them expecting.
