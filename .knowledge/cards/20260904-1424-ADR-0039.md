## Decision

A manifest loaded from the database is trusted exactly as one loaded from
disk. Two frozensets change:

| Constant | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `_TRUSTED_SOURCES` (`backend/agents/workflows/compiler.py:212`) | `{"file", "builtin"}` | `{"file", "builtin", "db"}` |
| `TRUSTED_TRUST_LEVELS` (`backend/agents/workflows/permission_caps.py:73`) | `{"file", "builtin"}` | `{"file", "builtin", "db"}` |

Consequences, all of them intended:

- A `db` manifest resolves to the **trusted** permission cap, so `exec` and
  `spawn_subagents` are permitted rather than AND-masked off by
  `intersect_permissions`.
- A `db` manifest may **raise** a `Limits` ceiling above
  `_LIMITS_DEFAULT_CEILING` (`max_subagents` 8, `max_depth` 2,
  `wall_clock_seconds` 900) instead of being refused with a `CompilerError`.
- CAP-03 no longer checks a `db` manifest's capability references against
  `user_allowed`, so trusted-only capabilities (`gate:security`,
  `gate:approval`, `runtime:langchain_deepagents`, `compaction:html_skeleton`)
  become reachable from a Composer-saved workflow.
- `user` is now the only untrusted source.

`backend/app/core/entitlements.py` gains `pdf_to_prototype` and
`sample_subagents_parallel` rows on the enterprise tier — `user_launchable:
true` alone does not entitle a pipeline.

## Why

The trust split answered "how much may this cost and what may it reach" with
"where do the bytes live". Those are unrelated questions, and keying one on
the other made the Composer structurally incapable of expressing the work it
exists to express: a `wave_scheduler` build charging one subagent per task
dies at task 9 under the untrusted ceiling, mid-run, with earlier waves
already written to disk. Moving the manifest to disk bought the budget only by
making it no longer user-editable — the exact property the Composer sells.

[ADR-0038](20260902-1321-ADR-0038.md) analyses this in full and proposes the
better fix: an admin-editable per-owner `resource_policy`, with budget and
tool tier as separate axes so "give this team more subagents" can never
silently become "user workflows may now shell out".

**This ADR is the crude alternative, and it is deliberately taking a cost that
ADR-0038 was designed to avoid.** It collapses the two axes ADR-0038 keeps
apart: a DB row now gains cost headroom and `exec`/`spawn_subagents` reach in
one move, because they ride the same `trusted` cap key. ADR-0038 remains the
intended destination; this does not implement any part of it and does not
reduce the work it describes.

It supersedes, and directly contradicts, two locked decisions recorded in
`.planning/IMPLEMENTATION-REGISTER.md`:

- **Phase 8, "Trust posture"** — *"A user/db manifest referencing a
  `user_allowed=False` cap → `CompilerError` naming `(kind,name)`;
  `exec`/`secrets`/`spawn_subagents` collapse OFF via `intersect_permissions`
  (AND-mask, can only LOWER)"*.
- **Phase 11, "Trust-conditional `Limits`"** — *"file/builtin manifests may
  RAISE a Limits cap; user/db manifests may only LOWER (raising any →
  `CompilerError`). `spawn_subagents` rides the SAME trust ceiling as `exec`
  (file may grant, user/db collapses OFF + CAP-03 rejects)"*.

Both statements are now false of the code. The register is a frozen archive
and is not edited; this card is the record that the posture changed.

## Residual risk

1. **The launch path did not move.** `backend/app/api/run_commands.py:2671`
   compiles an INLINE manifest at `trust="user"` and a saved row at
   `trust="db"`. Only saved rows gained privilege. A client that posts a
   manifest inline at launch is still fully constrained — correct, but it
   means "the workflow ran from the Composer" and "the workflow ran from an
   inline body" now have materially different authorization.
2. **No runtime backstop exists.** The SecurityGate runtime trust check
   recorded as IN-02 (`.planning/IMPLEMENTATION-REGISTER.md:1079`) still
   cannot fire, leaving the compiler as the only place manifest trust is
   enforced. Before this change a DB row had two weak guards; it now has one.
3. **The blast radius is every saved row, retroactively.** Trust is evaluated
   at compile time, not at save time, so workflows saved months ago under the
   old posture are re-compiled under the new one on their next run. Nothing
   re-validates them and no migration marks them.
4. **`user_allowed=False` no longer means what it says** for the db path. The
   flag is still honoured for `user`, so the registry's own documentation is
   now accurate for one of two untrusted-looking sources.
