Overview of Subsea Fibre Splicing

Subsea fibre splicing is the critical process of permanently joining optical cable segments on the seafloor or during installation operations.

Key objectives:

  • Maintain optical signal integrity across junction points
  • Achieve low splice loss (typically <0.1 dB)
  • Ensure mechanical strength matching parent cable
  • Provide environmental protection and pressure resistance
  • Enable efficient cable deployment and repair workflows

Splice Loss Targets and Performance Metrics

Standard performance benchmarks for subsea splice operations:

Splice Loss Target
<0.1 dB per joint
Return Loss
>50 dB
Mechanical Strength
≥95% of parent cable tensile rating
Environmental Seal
Pressure-rated to 600+ bar
Mean Time Between Failures
>25 years

These metrics ensure cable reliability over the operational lifetime and accommodate environmental stressors including hydrostatic pressure, thermal cycling, and corrosion.

Pre-Splice Inspection and Preparation

Critical pre-operation steps:

1. Cable segment verification

  • Visual inspection for damage, coating integrity, kinks
  • Measure cable dimensions and confirm type/spec compatibility

2. Fiber end preparation

  • Remove protective layers and buffer tubes
  • Cleave fiber ends at precise 90° angle using mechanical cleaver
  • Inspect cleave surfaces under magnification (100× minimum)

3. Environmental controls

  • Minimize dust and contamination in splice chamber
  • Monitor temperature and humidity (typically 18–28°C, <60% RH)
  • Verify lighting and work station ergonomics

Core Splicing Techniques

Two primary subsea splicing methods:

Fusion Splicing (preferred for reliability)

  • Electrodes heat fiber ends to melting point (1,300–2,000°C)
  • Automated alignment and welding within fusion splicer
  • Splice loss typically 0.02–0.05 dB
  • Permanent, low-maintenance joint

Mechanical Splicing (field repair/temporary)

  • Precision alignment sleeves and clamps
  • No heat required; manual positioning
  • Splice loss typically 0.1–0.2 dB
  • Faster deployment; reversible

Recommendation: Fusion splicing standard for new installations; mechanical available for emergency repairs.

Splice Equipment and Preparation

Essential Equipment Checklist

  • Fusion splicer (automated arc-discharge type)
  • Precision fiber cleaver
  • Magnification inspection system (100–400×)
  • Protective splicing sleeves (heat-shrink or mechanical)
  • Optical power meter and continuity tester
  • Grounding and static-protection equipment

In-Field Splice Protection

  • Heat-shrink sleeves (primary mechanical protection)
  • Reinforced buffer tubes for secondary isolation
  • Pressure-rated splice boxes for subsea recovery
  • Epoxy gel for water-blocking and elasticity

Quality Assurance and Testing

Post-splice validation checklist:

  • Optical loss verification (OTDR or power meter method)
  • Visual inspection of splice geometry and alignment
  • Mechanical tensile strength test (if protocol requires, up to 500 kg pull force)
  • Environmental chamber cycling (thermal/pressure shock simulation)
  • Continuity and impedance measurement
  • Documentation and batch traceability records

Acceptance Criteria:

  • Loss within specification (<0.1 dB for production, <0.2 dB for field repairs)
  • No visible defects, cracks, or voids in protective layers
  • Tensile strength >95% baseline
  • All test records archived for 25+ year lifecycle

Post-Splice Operations and Cable Recovery

1. Protective layer assembly

  • Seal splice chamber with pressure-tested housing
  • Apply hydrophobic gel for water exclusion
  • Apply secondary mechanical protection (armoring if required)

2. Cable routing and stability

  • Position spliced section in stable lay orientation
  • Secure cable to seabed if required by design (burial, trenching, or weight)
  • Verify cable path alignment and strain relief

3. Final deployment verification

  • Confirm optical continuity across full system
  • Document splice location and coordinates
  • Establish handoff to operations and maintenance teams

4. Maintenance planning

  • Schedule periodic re-testing at 1, 5, 10, 25-year intervals
  • Monitor optical performance trends
  • Plan contingency repair procedures

Safety Protocols and Risk Mitigation

Critical safety practices:

High-voltage hazards

  • Fusion splicer operates at 5–10 kV; use grounding protocols
  • Never work on live circuits; verify circuit isolation first

Pressurized equipment

  • Subsea splice housings rated to >600 bar; handle carefully
  • Use certified pressure test equipment only
  • Train personnel on hyperbaric environments if on-site work required

Environmental factors

  • Monitor weather windows for surface or nearshore operations
  • Use anti-static mats and grounding straps in work area
  • Maintain clean fiber handling protocols to prevent contamination

Emergency procedures

  • Establish repair protocols for failed splices (backup splicer on-site)
  • Maintain redundant communication systems
  • Document all incidents and near-misses
